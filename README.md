# San Andreas browser game

An in-progress single-player browser recreation focused on native San Andreas content and presentation, with no application backend.

[Play on GitHub Pages](https://sir-teo.github.io/web-gta/). The game opens one continuous San Andreas world at Grove Street and streams native terrain as you travel. Separate district choices and their old public packages have been removed. Desktop Chrome with a keyboard and mouse plays best; phones, tablets and game controllers play too (see [Playing on a phone or tablet](#playing-on-a-phone-or-tablet)).

The static content includes all 16,297 archive entries and 212 vehicle definitions. The vehicle audit resolves all 212 models, including four corrupt depot sources repaired at load (coordinate sanitize, box-collision fallback). A native Greenwood is drivable near the starting point. Fixed-model car spawns now load nearby from the original world records, with original models, paint, collision and handling. Seventeen native aircraft are flyable, including nine helicopters and the Hydra with switchable VTOL. A bounded civilian population walks native sidewalk links nearby. Up to three ambient cars follow nearby native road links, brake for obstacles and traffic lights, and stop before unloaded road. This is an initial browser traffic controller; native population budgets drive density (36/44 device clamp) and the original script runs the story from its first mission; traffic now has seated native drivers. See [world content](docs/static-world-content.md).

WASD moves, Shift sprints, Space jumps, C crouches, holding right mouse to target a Families member and pressing G or H recruits him into your crew (they shadow you, pile into passenger seats and share your attackers); with a crew, G tells it to follow and H to stay in position, and holding either for two seconds disbands it; Y or N says something positive or negative to the pedestrian in front of CJ (or the one targeted with right mouse), who turns and answers, while an insulted rival gang member or tough guy squares up and comes for him and a timid one runs; drag looks around, V/Home changes camera distance, and Escape pauses. M opens the original full map; click it or choose a named destination to set a waypoint. The minimap tracks roads, your heading and the radar's blips; the full map shows the same blips under the original's exploration fog. With Unarmed selected, R punches nearby pedestrians in front of you. Punches and moving-car impacts knock pedestrians down; they recover and flee, and nearby witnesses flee too. These are simplified gameplay reactions using native animations, with wanted levels, pursuing armed police and native combat audio; ragdolls remain unfinished. F enters/exits the nearest car, boat, bike or aircraft (passing over a bike a script ped rides); moving traffic must stop before you can take over, and a car that has stopped for CJ in the road or at a light waits while he goes round to its door; a pulled-out driver lands on the ground and gets up before fleeing or coming back for his car; W/S accelerates/brakes/reverses, A/D steers, Space applies the handbrake and H (or Caps Lock) sounds the horn; tap it in a police car to toggle the siren. Bikes pedal with W/S, lean into A/D steering and throw the rider on hard impacts. Ctrl fires a drive-by from the driven car or boat with the Uzi, Tec-9 or MP5 the driver takes up at the wheel (R reloads); The original wheels now roll, steer and follow suspension. Entry/exit is immediate; native seated driver poses are implemented, and vehicle, aircraft and weather sounds run through a port of the game's sound manager (docs/research/NATIVE_AUDIO.md). Weapon damage can disable cars and reveal native damaged body components. Rendering and footstep audio use native assets. The authored sandbox, procedural character and car stand-ins, synthetic audio and sandbox save fixtures have been deleted.

A new game starts CJ with the whole arsenal loaded and full body armour, and INTRO's closing weapon clear-out hands them back (the original starts him with nothing). **Tab opens the arsenal**, with all 44 handheld weapon/equipment IDs unlocked, native models, HUD icons and use animations. Right mouse aims (with a gun it also drops CJ out of a sprint into the aimed walk); left mouse while aiming, or Ctrl, fires/uses the selected item. R reloads guns (with native reload animations for ten supported weapons) or swings the equipped melee weapon; Q and E or the mouse wheel cycle weapons. The arsenal includes an ammunition refill button. Firearms damage civilians and cars; rockets, grenades, remote satchels, fire and gas have distinct behaviors. Select the detonator to trigger placed satchels. Camera use downloads a PNG; night/thermal goggles toggle their view; use the parachute while airborne to slow descent. Ammo, effects and civilian/traffic movement pause with menus. Weapon tuning and aim correction are approximate browser implementations; dual wielding, stealth executions and the remaining reload animations remain unfinished. Confirmed hits improve weapon practice, reducing reload time and spread. Wanted levels bring foot police, whose rounds go astray in proportion to their inaccuracy (street cops miss more often than SWAT, the FBI or the army); siren cars flash red and blue coronas from their light bars: at one star they club an unarmed suspect with the nightstick and bust him once he is knocked down. See [weapon implementation](docs/research/PLAYABLE_WEAPONS.md).

Open **M → Aircraft → Fly from Verdant Meadows** to start on the original airfield in the same world. W/S adjusts throttle, ↓/↑ raises/lowers the nose, A/D banks, Q/E steers the rudder, G toggles retractable gear, and Space brakes on the ground. F exits after landing and stopping. The flight HUD shows airspeed, height above ground, throttle and gear. Nearby supported planes also spawn at original IPL locations. Flight uses authored flight equations with native assets and source handling coefficients (stall authority, ground effect, crosswind coupling); helicopters and Hydra VTOL use an authored lift controller. Space stabilizes hover, T switches Hydra flight mode, Ctrl fires mounted guns and B launches rockets on supported military aircraft. Collision and weapon damage can destroy aircraft. Helicopter searchlight pursuit, rotor-gated flight response and pilot seating run through the port; full-mission flight choreography remains unfinished. See [aircraft implementation](docs/research/PLAYABLE_AIRCRAFT.md).

Money, ammunition, the weapons held, weapon and driving skill, collected pickups, sprayed tags, stunt jumps, explored map squares and the last save point save locally; a game started after a save point was used skips the introduction and resumes there, and the menu then offers New Game (with the original's confirmation) to start over.

See the [remaining gaps checklist](docs/GAPS_CHECKLIST.md) for implemented features and outstanding work.

## Playing on a phone or tablet

Touch controls appear over the game on a phone or tablet, drawn after the iOS and Android port's and laid out by its own default layout files, the one it would load for the device: `TouchDefaultPhoneWidescreen.cfg` on a phone, `TouchDefaultTablet4x3.cfg` on an iPad (or `TouchDefaultTabletWidescreen.cfg` when the page is at least 3:2 across, as under Safari's bars), and `TouchDefaultPhone3x2.cfg` on a narrower phone. Each button stands at its widget's place on the port's 640×448 screen (on a phone Sprint large in the bottom right corner with attack, enter and the phone's place above it; the large Gas and Brake pedals, handbrake, drive-by fire and a small horn at the wheel; the steering arrows bottom left; Skip in the top right) and is as big as the port draws it: its SMALL, MEDIUM, LARGE and LARGER are 17, 24, 29 and 32 on the 640 in the iOS executable, a button sized by the screen's width up to 16:9 (the port ran letterboxed on a wider iPhone). Buttons the port has no widget for stand in free places of its grid. Add `?touch=1` to the address to force them on or `?touch=0` to hide them.

- **Starting:** starting the game takes the screen fullscreen and holds it sideways where the browser allows. Safari on an iPhone cannot go fullscreen, so the menu suggests Share → Add to Home Screen, which opens the game without Safari's bars; where Android's browser offers to install the game, the menu shows Add to Home screen, and the installed game opens full screen and sideways. While the game plays the screen is kept on. The back button or gesture (or leaving fullscreen) closes the map or the arsenal, or else pauses, rather than leaving the page.
- **On foot:** a floating stick under the left thumb (a light push walks), a drag on the right to look, and icon buttons for Fire (showing the weapon in hand), Aim, Jump, Sprint, Enter, Crouch and Reload; Enter shows only beside or in a vehicle. Aim with a gun eases the crosshair onto the ped nearest it, as a pad's lock-on does; a drag takes the camera back, and a swipe onto another ped switches to him. Through a scope Jump and Sprint become Zoom + and Zoom −; in the water the buttons are Dive, Swim and Jump.
- **Weapons:** the HUD's weapon icon is tapped for the next weapon, swiped left for the one before, or held for the arsenal.
- **Driving:** Gas and Brake pedals under the right thumb, so the stick steers to full lock, with H-brake, Horn, Radio (where the vehicle has one: not a bicycle) and Look back; ◀ Look and Look ▶ come up while a drive-by can be fired. A car fitted with nitro gets a Nitrous button and a side-mission vehicle (taxi, ambulance, fire truck, police vehicle...) a Mission button. A passenger keeps Fire, Look back, Radio and Enter. The first time at the wheel the game asks how to steer.
- **Flying:** the stick is the yoke, with Thrust, Slow, Brake, ◀ Yaw, Yaw ▶ and Rocket under the right thumb, Gear above them in the air for a plane that retracts it, and VTOL in the Hydra.
- **Scenes and prompts:** during a cutscene or a scripted scene in the widescreen bars (the intro's ride) the controls give way, to Skip when it can be skipped. Help text names the touch buttons, each led by its button's glyph as the port's help draws the widget; when it asks for one with no button of its own (Yes, No, Action to collect a pickup, Mission, Follow, Stay, Look back) that button appears above the others. A trip skip's icon is pressed to take it.
- **Menus and the map:** a shop's or garage's script menu takes a tap on its lines (one lights a line, another chooses it), with ▲ ▼ Select and Back (◀ ▶ for a colour grid) under the left thumb, after the port's arrow widgets, and its help names them. The radar in the top left corner opens the map, which pinches and drags; under the radar sit the pause button and a camera button, which steps the view as V does. The pause menu's Brief lists the mission text shown lately (the front end's Brief page, kept by the game for every player), newest at the bottom, for a line that flashed past.
- **Settings** (the pause menu's Controls panel, after the port's): steering by the stick, by the port's left and right arrow buttons or by tilting the device; lock-on aim assist or free aim; look speed and invert look; button size; vibration; resolution (High draws at the screen's density, up to twice the default, sharper and slower; Low at three quarters, softer and quicker); population (Full, Medium, the touch default, or Low: how many peds and cars are kept about, which a phone's CPU feels); and Adjust controls, which drags the buttons where your thumbs want them and sizes the one touched last (− and +, 60 to 160%), on foot, driving and flying, kept for the next visit.
- **Game controllers:** an MFi, Xbox or PlayStation pad over Bluetooth (or any the browser maps as standard) plays with the PS2 original's layout, as the iOS and Android port's does: the left stick walks and steers, the right stick looks, cross sprints (accelerates at the wheel), square jumps (brakes), triangle enters and exits, circle attacks, R1 targets (the handbrake), L2 and R2 step the weapon (look left and right), select changes the view and Start pauses, and its D-pad and cross work the menus. Through a scope square and cross zoom; flying, circle fires the rockets and L1 the guns. While it plays the help names its buttons (a PlayStation pad's shapes, otherwise the Xbox letters), drawing the face buttons as the pad's own coloured symbols as the PS2 original's help did, and the touch buttons stand aside, until a key, a click or a touch.

## Development

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Production files are generated with `npm run build` and served by static hosting. GitHub Pages deployments use `BASE_PATH=/web-gta/`.

The [content starting point](docs/research/CONTENT_START_HERE.md) and [scene provenance](public/play/README.md) document content acquisition and packaging. [Design](docs/DESIGN.md), [implementation plan](docs/IMPLEMENTATION.md), and [research register](docs/research/SOURCES.md) track the larger objective. Older progress records describe historical prototypes and are not claims about current shipped behavior.

`npm run port -- census` inventories every command of the original script against this port and gta-reversed and ranks what to port next; `npm run port -- sweep 2,11` runs missions headless and reports where each stops (see the [porting pipeline](docs/port/README.md), its [status](docs/port/STATUS.md) and the [roadmap for the remaining missions](docs/port/ROADMAP.md)). A command whose engine side is not ported yet is skipped (its outputs read as nothing found) rather than ending the mission thread.

The native texture/model workshops remain at `assets.html` and `models.html`. They support local native assets, character assembly, animation inspection, scene assembly and temporary playable scene sessions. The game pause menu links to the character workshop. Files are processed locally in the browser.

## Verification

```sh
npm test
npm run test:browser
npm run test:production
node scripts/verify-pages.mjs https://sir-teo.github.io/web-gta/
```

Browser tests use locally installed Chrome and an owned development server on port 5189; they run on three parallel workers (`PW_WORKERS=1 npm run test:browser` for a serial run), then the load-sensitive specs listed in `playwright.config.ts` as a second pass, and only one `playwright test` may run at a time since it owns that port. `ROUTE='{...}' npx playwright test tests/browser/route-drive.spec.ts` drives a scripted route and reports streaming latency and holds. The unit suite keeps its module transforms across runs. The production check serves a build under `/web-gta/`, verifies native loading, movement and pause/resume, and rejects HTTP failures and browser exceptions. Screenshots are written to the ignored `artifacts/` directory.

Walking physics tests use a minimal flat-floor fixture. Native movement, collision and vehicle algorithms remain available for content integration.
